# Three run-throughs

These numbers come from the engine (`npm test`). They are not typed by hand and then reverse-fitted.

Household expenses for Priya include her ₹28,000 rent plus ordinary living (₹42,000 all-in). Ravi’s shop household spend is ₹22,000. Anita’s existing app EMIs are entered as ₹8,500 a month on ₹35,000 still due at 32% — the brief did not give an EMI rupee, so that is my translation of “three app loans, ₹35,000 outstanding at 30%+”.

## Priya, 29 · Bengaluru · salaried

Wants ₹8,00,000 personal loan for a wedding.

### Questions the app asked

**Must** — 9 questions: purpose, amount, product guess, age, how you earn, take-home, existing EMIs, household spend, score.

**Extra (salaried path — no ITR, no shop)**

| Question | Her answer | What it moved |
|---|---|---|
| What kind of company pays you? | Large multinational | Rate −0.50pp, multiple |
| How many years in this job? | 5 | Rate −0.25pp |
| Months of savings? | 4 | No thin-savings haircut |
| Months left on the car loan? | 24 | Wait-vs-now story |

She can skip this whole extra list after the must-set and still get a brief (wider bands). She never saw: ITR, GST, shop value, gig cash, bonus share, card utilisation.

### Four outputs

| | |
|---|---|
| **O1** | **Borrow less.** A wedding should not sit on the last rupee of the ceiling. |
| **O2 lender** | ₹14.5 L – ₹17.9 L. A prime MNC file can print a fat personal loan. |
| **O2 household** | ₹7.6 L – ₹8.3 L. **Use ₹7.3 L.** |
| **O3** | Fair headline **10.8% – 12.8%**. All-in APR **11.7% – 13.7%** after 1.5% fee + GST. |
| **O4** | Do not agree to more than **₹21,760** a month (48-month tenor). |

**Why ₹21,760 and not ₹30,000.** Household FOIR 36% of ₹1,10,000 is ₹39,600. Minus the car EMI of ₹14,000 leaves ₹25,600. Then a 15% wedding haircut. That leftover is the ceiling, not a 50% sanction letter.

**Wait vs now.** The car EMI ends in about 24 months. Waiting is a real option if the wedding can move.

**Stress.** Income −25% or rate +250 bps. A wedding ticket at the top of the band is fragile. Keep the household number.

### Walk-in card (what she says)

> Fair for my profile on a personal loan is 10.8–12.8% (all-in APR about 11.7–13.7%). I will not agree to an EMI above ₹21,760, or a ticket above ₹7,30,000. You may still sanction ₹14–18 lakh. That is your grid. It is not what my kitchen can carry.

If they quote 14%: the offer checker tags it **a bit high / expensive**. Ask them to match 12.8% or explain the extra on the Key Facts Statement.

## Ravi, 42 · Mysuru · kirana

Wants ₹15,00,000 for a second stock line and a delivery vehicle. He walked in thinking “personal loan”.

### Questions the app asked

**Must** — cash path, not salary (9 questions): typical month ₹40,000, existing EMI ₹0, household spend ₹22,000, score **I do not know**.

**Extra (shop path — no MNC, no card utilisation)**

Vintage 14 years, ITR ₹4,20,000, shop ₹45,00,000, wife ₹18,000, extra earn claimed ₹18,000.

The demo persona also carries GST-registered = yes as a known fact from the assignment brief (not an extra question). He never saw: employer type, bonus share, card utilisation, bounce, savings (thin-savings haircut does not apply to a LAP).

### Four outputs

| | |
|---|---|
| **O1** | **Borrow** — on a **LAP**, not the personal loan he asked for. |
| **O2 lender** | ₹16.7 L – ₹24.0 L (LTV on the shop, FOIR on ITR + co-applicant). |
| **O2 household** | ₹14.8 L – ₹16.6 L. **Use ₹15,00,000** — the ask fits. |
| **O3** | **10.5% – 13.8%**. Score unknown, so this is a **wide** band that prices the shop, not a fake 300. All-in APR **10.8% – 14.1%**. Confidence capped at **56%** because there is no bureau. |
| **O4** | EMI ceiling **₹25,000** on a 108-month tenor. |

**Why LAP.** Unencumbered shop worth ₹45 lakh, ask ₹15 lakh, business purpose. Unsecured would be smaller and costlier. The card says: do not walk in for a personal loan.

**Two books.** Lender uses ITR (₹35,000 a month) plus GST lift plus the wife in full as co-applicant. The house uses a slow cash month (₹40,000) plus 90% of her pay plus 35% of the extra the stock line is supposed to earn. Those are supposed to disagree.

**Thin savings.** Two months of savings would have haircut an unsecured ticket. On a LAP the shop is the airbag, so I do not apply the same 30% cut. That is a rule I will defend.

### Walk-in card (what he says)

> Fair for my profile on a loan against my shop is 10.5–13.8% (all-in APR about 10.8–14.1%). I will take ₹15,00,000. I will not agree to an EMI above ₹25,000. You may sanction more against the shop. I still use ₹15 lakh. I do not know my score — if you pull a bureau and it is clean, the low end of this band is the number I want on paper.

## Anita, 35 · Hubballi · gig + tailoring

Wants ₹1,50,000 for an electric scooter to double delivery runs.

### Questions the app asked

**Must** — informal typical month ₹26,000, existing EMI ₹8,500, spend ₹18,000, score unknown.

**Extra (thin-file path)** — people you support who do not earn: two children and husband out of work, 0.5 months savings, **bounce yes**, existing rate **32%**.

She never saw: MNC vs government, ITR, shop LTV, extra-earn forecast.

### Four outputs

| | |
|---|---|
| **O1** | **Don’t borrow.** An EMI already bounced, and she is paying 32% on existing debt. A new loan is more fuel. |
| **O2 lender** | **₹70,000 – ₹1,20,000** as the sales pitch a scooter counter may still try to book. |
| **O2 household** | **₹0 – ₹0. Use ₹0.** |
| **O3** | Informal two-wheeler band **16.0% – 21.5%**, APR **17.7% – 23.3%**. Irrelevant if she should not sign. |
| **O4** | EMI ceiling **₹0**. |

**Why ₹0 and not ₹1,50,000.** After food, two children, an unemployed adult, a ₹8,000 buffer floor plus dependents, and ₹8,500 of app EMIs, leftover cash is negative. Household FOIR 28% cannot open a new ticket. The bounce + 32% rule fires don’t-borrow even before leftover cash is discussed.

**Why we still show ₹70k–₹1.2L on the lender side.** Hiding the pitch would leave her unarmed tomorrow. The card says that number is a sales pitch, not a household number.

**What would change this.** Gold jewellery to refinance the 32% paper; husband earning again; the app loans closed. Doubling delivery runs is a real idea. Financing it on top of bounced payday credit is how the idea fails.

### Walk-in card (what she says)

> Fair for me is not a new loan. If you still quote ₹70,000–₹1,20,000 on a scooter, that is a sales pitch, not a household number. I will not take a new EMI. If I borrow at all, it is only to kill the 32% app loans, not to add a vehicle on top.

## What the three together prove

- Lender amount and household amount are different for all three. Priya could be sold ~₹16 lakh and should use ~₹7.3 lakh. Ravi could be sold more LAP than ₹15 lakh and should still use ₹15 lakh. Anita could be sold a scooter and should use ₹0.
- Don’t borrow fires (Anita). Borrow less fires (Priya). Borrow fires (Ravi) only after the product is made honest (LAP).
- Unknown score (Ravi, Anita) is a wide band with a confidence cap. It is not 300.
- Adaptive questions: three different lists.
- APR is above headline once fee + GST are in.
